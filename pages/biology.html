Lung Fibrosis

Lung fibrosis is characterized by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix components, leading to irreversible tissue remodeling and loss of pulmonary function. It commonly arises in the context of chronic inflammation or repeated tissue injury.

Innate Immune Cells in Lung Tissue

Macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells play essential roles in immune surveillance, inflammation, and tissue repair. Under pathological conditions, these cells may adopt activation states that promote fibrosis.

Pro-fibrotic Activation States

Pro-fibrotic immune states are defined by transcriptional programs that favor extracellular matrix production, fibroblast activation, and sustained inflammatory signaling.
