LungFib scRNA-seq

Single-cell RNA sequencing study of innate immune cell states in lung fibrosis, with emphasis on transcriptional programs and regulatory mechanisms underlying pro-fibrotic activation.

Project Overview

In lung tissue, chronic inflammation or injury can drive fibrosis through aberrant activation of innate immune cells such as macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells. While fibrosis-associated pathways have been studied at the tissue level, the cell-type–specific transcriptional programs and transcription factors governing pro-fibrotic immune states remain incompletely characterized.

This project employs single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to resolve immune heterogeneity in lung tissue, identify immune subpopulations most affected during fibrosis, characterize their gene expression profiles, and infer regulatory transcription factors and pathways associated with fibrotic remodeling.

Scientific Motivation

Fibrosis is a progressive and often irreversible pathological process underlying multiple chronic lung diseases. Innate immune cells play a central role in coordinating inflammatory and reparative responses; however, prolonged or dysregulated activation can promote excessive extracellular matrix deposition and tissue scarring.

The key challenge is not the absence of fibrosis-associated genes, but the lack of cell-type–resolved understanding of the regulatory programs driving pro-fibrotic immune states.

By leveraging scRNA-seq, this project aims to bridge this gap by linking immune cell identity, transcriptional state, and regulatory control.

Project Objectives

  • Identify innate immune cell subpopulations associated with pro-fibrotic activation
  • Characterize transcriptional signatures at single-cell resolution
  • Explore fibrosis-related pathways and candidate regulatory transcription factors
  • Develop a reproducible, tutorial-style scRNA-seq analysis workflow
  • Lay the foundation for a publication-oriented computational study

Methodological Roadmap

Phase I: Foundations

The project begins with developing a strong foundation in computational genomics and single-cell analysis. Initial learning is guided by structured online resources, including an introductory book on computational genomics with R.

Phase II: scRNA-seq Analysis

Building on these foundations, the project progresses to hands-on scRNA-seq analysis using R and Seurat, covering quality control, normalization, dimensionality reduction, clustering, and cell-type annotation.

Phase III: Biological Interpretation and Regulation

Advanced stages focus on biological interpretation, pathway analysis, and inference of transcriptional regulators implicated in fibrotic processes, informed by existing literature and prior studies.
